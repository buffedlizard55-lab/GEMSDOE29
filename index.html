What is known about the reported 0.2600 raster?

The local deterministic dot_thin(H19-5, 2.8) reconstruction has 44,090 positive pixels and matches the owner-mirrored D2.8 pixel mask exactly. The GeoTIFF bytes differ. This verifies mask reproduction, not the score-to-file association. The 0.2600 score remains owner-reported; it appears at rank 15 in the public leaderboard, but no organizer receipt ties that mirror to the score.

Plausible mechanism, not causal proof: the registered minimum spacing is 2.8 px (~280 m); the locally pinned template resolution is 100 m and the official metric's linear distance-support radius is 300 m. Its false-positive and false-negative weights are 0.2 and 0.8, respectively. The metric grants distance-weighted credit near mapped faults while penalizing prediction mass farther away. A sparse subset can plausibly remove redundant or remote pixels while retaining enough nearby line coverage; a spacing near the support scale makes that trade-off worth testing, but does not guarantee good alignment or score.

For context, the owner-reported H19-5 parent has 121,131 pixels / score 0.1922. The D1.5 and D2.8 variants have 60,069 and 44,090 pixels, with reported scores 0.2477 and 0.2600 (difference +0.0123). D2.8 is about 63.6% fewer pixels than the parent (D1.5: 50.4%). These within-family public-score claims are consistent with geometric budget calibration being important, but they do not prove why any score changed. The local comparison and provenance qualifications are in the manifest, score registry, and reproduction test.
