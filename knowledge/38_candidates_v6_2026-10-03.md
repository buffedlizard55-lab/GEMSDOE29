## Rank 1 — H53: cross-scale topographic fabric coherence (scale-persistent orientation, label-free)

- **Layers:** `det_elev`, `det_elev_slope` (cached 100 m bands), the LiDAR scarp band behind `L_*`;
  the supplied catalogue only for the off-catalogue mask. **No external data.**
- **Physical signature:** the structure-tensor orientation *coherence* of the DEM gradient at three
  window scales (3 / 7 / 15 px = 300 / 700 / 1500 m) and the **agreement of the dominant strike across
  those scales**. A fault-controlled lineament keeps one strike across two orders of window size; dune
  fields, drainage noise and cultural grids rotate as the window grows. Candidate columns: the minimum
  coherence across the three scales, the number of scales whose strikes agree within ±15°, and the
  product of the two.
- **Why it can catch a fault the catalogue lacks:** a blind strand under alluvium loses its scarp
  amplitude but keeps a weak, *scale-persistent* fabric; amplitude-thresholded scarp filters drop exactly
  those pixels. This targets the population "newly mapped geometry of an existing system".
- **Difference from repo work:** every orientation column in the repository is single-scale or
  label-derived — `L_strike_c2`/`L_strike_s2` are one-scale LiDAR strikes and `L_coh100` is a ~2 km
  resultant length computed **from the visible catalogue mask** (`src/gemsdoe/features.py:243-256`,
  family E), i.e. it cannot see anything the catalogue does not already contain. H53 is label-free and
  its unit is *scale-persistence*, not amplitude. Greps: `cross-scale` and `multiscale orientation` have
  **zero** hits in `src/`, `scripts/`, `tests/`.
- **Cost / rank justification:** zero downloads, ~0.5 day (three structure-tensor passes + agreement +
  synthetic tests). Bracket **0 to +0.012**; ranked first because it sits in the only physics family
  with a replicated 8/8 inclusion effect and adds an axis (scale) that family has never used.
- **Known risks:** 100 m DEM coarsens the 300 m window; lithologic layering (bedding, dyke swarms) also
  produces scale-persistent fabric, so the arm must isolate the *off-catalogue straight-segment*
  increment, not raw coherence.

## Rank 2 — H50 (carried forward from v5 rank 1, still unbuilt): range-front segmentation, Smf per front

- **Layers:** `det_elev` + `det_elev_slope`, family-B curvature for front extraction, catalogue only for
  the off-catalogue mask. **No external data.**
- **Physical signature:** mountain-front sinuosity **Smf = Lmf / Ls** (length of the range–piedmont
  junction over its straight-line length) computed per range-front segment, with segment relief and a
  segment-validity mask. Straight, high-relief segments (Smf ≈ 1.0–1.4) are fault-controlled active
  fronts; embayed low-relief segments (Smf > 3) are erosional. Definition and activity classes: Bull &
  McFadden (1977), as reviewed in Silva et al. (2003, *Geomorphology* 35, 17–34,
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169555X02002155) and Keller & Pinter (2002,
  *Active Tectonics*, 2nd ed.); "Smf < 1.4 indicates tectonically active fronts" (Keller 1986, quoted in
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/285368808_Mountain_Fronts).
- **Why off-catalogue:** the catalogue maps strands, not front activity. A straight 5–15 km front with
  no mapped trace is an unmapped strand holding the front up; local-window scarp filters see the step but
  not the *segment*, and H43's knickpoints see the drainage response but not the front planform.
- **Difference from repo work:** no range-front, sinuosity, Smf or faceted-spur computation exists
  anywhere in `src/`, `scripts/`, `tests/` (grep: `sinuosity`, `Smf` → zero hits). H50's unit is the
  front segment — the largest spatial scale the project has not tested.
- **Cost:** zero downloads, ~0.5–1 day (front extraction is the risky part). Bracket **0 to +0.012**.
  Ranked second only because front extraction on a 100 m DEM has more failure modes than three
  structure-tensor passes.
- **Known risks:** pediment/volcanic-cover fronts have no meaningful Smf (hence the validity mask); Smf
  also tracks lithology, so the arm design must isolate the off-catalogue straight-segment increment.

## Rank 3 — H54: gravity-profile asymmetry (a fault step, not a symmetric source)

- **Layers:** `iso_grav_anom` (band 13), `iso_grav_anom_hg` (band 18), `depth_to_base_surf` (band 15).
  **No external data.**
- **Physical signature:** along the across-strike direction, the **skewness** (third standardized
  moment) of the isostatic-gravity profile in a ~1.5 km window, weighted by the across-strike gradient,
  plus the sign (which side is downthrown). A normal fault offsetting basement beneath basin fill gives
  a step-like, strongly asymmetric profile; an intrusion, diapir or fold gives a near-symmetric one.
- **Why off-catalogue:** the A family (`A_mag_as`, `A_mag_tilt`, `A_grav_as`, `A_grav_tilt`,
  `A_mag_hg_ridge`, `A_grav_hg_ridge` in the frozen static block) measures amplitude, tilt and ridge
  strength. None of those can distinguish a fault step from a symmetric body — which is precisely the
  question a mapper asks before drawing a blind fault under cover.
- **Difference from repo work:** greps for `skew` and `asymmetr` return **zero** hits in `src/`,
  `scripts/`, `tests/`. This is the third-moment statistic, not another amplitude transform.
- **Cost:** ~0.5 day (rotate windows to the local strike, then moments). Bracket **0 to +0.008**.
- **Known risks:** the isostatic residual already removes regional trend, so the residual step may be
  small; the window must be large enough to contain both blocks or the skewness measures noise.

## Rank 4 — H56: alteration-halo elongation matched to candidate strike

- **Layers:** `geodawn_rad_u8` (K, Th, U, TC) and `geodawn_extensions_u8` (Th/K, U/K, U/Th, TMI_up150),
  both already hash-pinned in `data/external/`. **No external data.**
- **Physical signature:** local covariance **anisotropy** of the K/Th anomaly field in a ~1 km window
  (major/minor eigenvalue ratio plus major-axis azimuth), delivered as anisotropy × cos²(angle between
  the elongation axis and the independently measured DEM/magnetic strike). Hydrothermal fluids migrate
  along structures, so alteration halos are *elongated along* the fault that plumbed them.
- **Why off-catalogue:** elongation is a pathway signature that survives burial and cover which erase
  scarps and mute magnetics; the supplied catalogue has no alteration channel at all.
- **Difference from repo work:** the D family uses radiometric ratio *pixels* (`D_K`, `D_Th`, `D_U`,
  `D_ThK`, `D_UK`, `D_UTh`, `D_ThK_edge`, `D_UK_edge`); v5's H49 would threshold *objects*; neither
  measures *shape anisotropy matched to strike*. Grep: `elongat` → zero hits; `anisotrop` appears only
  in `src/gemsdoe/h41.py` (an anisotropic scarp-strike corridor, a different construction).
- **Cost:** ~0.5 day. Bracket **0 to +0.008**.
- **Known risks:** radiometrics sense only the top ~30–50 cm, so "alteration" competes with soil and
  lithology; a frozen percentile rule for what counts as an anomaly must be declared before any fit.

## Rank 5 — H59: independent-layer edge-coincidence census

- **Layers:** DEM slope/curvature, RTP magnetics, isostatic gravity, radiometric TC, the LiDAR scarp
  band and MT conductance (`cond_surf`) — all local and hash-pinned. **No external data.**
- **Physical signature:** per-pixel **census** of how many *independent* datasets show an edge within
  300 m (integer 0–6) plus the fraction of layer pairs whose strikes agree within ±15°. Delivered as a
  census field, not as concatenated columns.
- **Why off-catalogue:** a blind structure that appears in four independent fields with a common strike
  is the classic structural mapper's argument for drawing an unmapped lineament.
- **Difference from repo work:** H35 used raw `D_ThK`/`D_UK` corroboration *pixels*; v5's H51 is
  agreement between two *model score* fields; the factorial's interaction terms are inside one model.
  Concatenating columns lets a boosted tree **substitute** one field for another; a census field makes
  coincidence an explicit requirement. Grep: no `census`/coincidence-count construction exists.
- **Cost:** ~1 day (six edge extractions + the census). Bracket **−0.003 to +0.010** — the only item
  with a negative lower bound, because a census can also collapse onto the catalogue's own corridors.
