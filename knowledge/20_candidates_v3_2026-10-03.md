## Rank 1 — H41: slip-rate-weighted INGENIOUS centroid corridors, off-catalogue only

* **Layers.** Hash-pinned mirror `data/external/gdr_qfaults_traces.csv` (GDR 1391 INGENIOUS Quaternary
  fault-trace *attributes*: 1,126 rows with `trace_id`, `slip_rate`, `recency`, `map_scale`, and grid
  centroid row/col; no geometry — recorded in `registry/data_manifest.json`). Corroborated against
  families A/B ridges; distance to the supplied catalogue masks out on-catalogue centroids.
* **Signature.** A sparse point field, not a raster edge: per-pixel value = Σ over *off-catalogue* centroids
  of `w = slip_rate·exp(-d/2.5 km)`, split into (i) raw support, (ii) support × local DEM-scarp ridge, and
  (iii) an anisotropic version smeared along the local scarp strike (corridor, not blob). The object is
  "a mapped-moving Quaternary trace exists near here, but no supplied-catalogue pixel does".
* **Why it can catch a fault missing from the catalogue.** INGENIOUS is the mapping program *producing*
  the Quaternary fault data the USGS catalogue has not yet fully absorbed inside the study window; its
  trace inventory (centroid attributes already mirrored here) is literally a catalogue of young
  faulting that post-dates the static supplied raster in places. The organizer statement that hidden
  labels include "newly mapped geometry of an existing fault system" makes near-trace habitat the
  single most on-mechanism prior available at zero download cost. (The CSV is attributes-only: corridors
  are 2.5-km-scale priors, not trace geometry — the honest ceiling.)
* **Difference from implemented work.** Family E is distance/orientation to the *supplied* catalogue;
  H35 uses only the supplied geometry. `D_gdr_dist_hot`/`D_gdr_kde2km` use wells/springs (thermal points),
  not fault-trace attributes with slip rate. Nothing in the reviewed repos touches the qfaults CSV at all.
* **Cost / validation.** ~0.5 day (data is local and hash-pinned); no external fetch needed. Two-proxy
  admission + G1/G2/G3 exactly as frozen for H35 (new draws 28/29 + confirmation 30/31, same
  `Cell` machinery). Planning bracket **+0.000 to +0.020**, low confidence (point prior may be too coarse
  at 100 m px; the 300 m kernel credit needs *dots on the fault*, and a centroid is at best a 2.5-km hint).
* **External data.** None required for the screen. Upgrade path (owner-side, unrestricted runner): the full
  INGENIOUS "Faults and Hostrocks" shapefile set from GDR 1391
  (<https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391>) would replace centroids with real trace geometry —
  obtainable (public, CC BY 4.0 per the release page transcribed in `registry/sources.json`), but not
  fetchable from this sandbox (IR-29-SANDBOX-NET), so the *upgraded* variant is only proposed as viable
  once mirrored with hashes.

## Rank 2 — H36 (unchanged, still untried): MT conductance structural edges + cross-fault asymmetry

Carried forward verbatim from v2 Rank 2: ridge of |∇cond_surf| with across-edge NMS, a discrete-step
asymmetry test (conductance step across the candidate edge, not a smooth gradient), coherence with a
potential-field lineament; layers `cond_surf`, `depth_to_base_surf`, `tc`, family-A ridges. Why off-catalogue:
MT sees through basin cover — blind faults carry a subsurface conductivity boundary with no scarp, so the
scarp-based catalogue cannot contain them. Difference: the factorial found family D *inert as raw bands*;
nobody has applied a structural-edge + asymmetry transform to it. Cost ~0.5–1 day, all inputs local
(hash-pinned competition stack). Bracket **0 to +0.010**, low confidence. Optional cross-check layer:
Peacock & Bedrosian 2022 conductance maps, DOI 10.5066/P9TWT2LU — metadata verified, download blocked in
sandbox (same IR as v2).

## Rank 3 — H37 (unchanged, still untried as specified): chemistry-geothermometry residual field

Carried forward from v2 Rank 3: residual of *chemistry-derived* reservoir temperature (quartz /
chalcedony / Na-K-Ca columns already tabulated in the mirrored GDR 1391 wells & springs CSV, hash
`122718e65bdf…` verified again this session) against a regional background, restricted to off-catalogue
lineament habitat, plus spring-cluster geometry. Mechanism: a heat anomaly with no mapped fault implies an
unmapped permeable pathway. Difference from tried work: H29-2 used the *2 m probe* temperature (failed);
the chemistry geothermometers have never been used as a spatial field anywhere in the family. Cost ~1 day;
inputs local. Bracket **0 to +0.015**, very low confidence (naive temperature version failed; the probe→
chemistry change of object is the entire bet).

## Rank 4 — H42: acquisition-line spectral audit with a *measured* period, then a one-shot notch

Refines v1/`hypotheses_next` H29-3 into an auditable sequence: (1) estimate the dominant cross-line
wavenumber per flight-line block from azimuthally banded power spectra of `mag_anom`/`rtp` (documented
spacing claims of 200/400 m flight lines are checked against the *measured* peak, never assumed);
(2) notch only if the peak exceeds the local continuum by a frozen margin; (3) recompute the A-family
ridges once on the de-lined grid and screen as a frozen column swap. Difference: H29's "line-orientation
audit" measured persistence by strike (finding E-W most persistent — registered as a refutation of the
naive expectation), and H31 registered drift; no reviewed repo performs a *spectral-domain* removal or a
measured-period audit. Cost ~1 day after the spectrum module is written; inputs local. Bracket
**0 to +0.008**, very low confidence, but note this is the only candidate that *improves every downstream
field* if the artifact is real — its value is partly as a data-quality receipt either way. The official
flight-path metadata (USGS GeoDAWN release, DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ) remains **not fetchable in the sandbox**;
the H29-3 variant that *assumed* spacing stays blocked pending that file (IR-29-SANDBOX-NET).

## Rank 5 — H38/H39 merged-downgrade: worm convergence size and Euler-depth consistency as *filters*

Kept from v2 Ranks 4–5 with one change of role after reading the H31 outcome: on this grid, persistence is
already tested twice (H29 corrected: all five arms failed; H31 seed-tracks: too sparse) and H40 tests the
dense surface. What has *not* been tried is using the continuation ladder as an **inclusion filter on the
emission itself** (drop dots whose 0-m edge has ≥N-pixel basin at the top of the ladder / whose Euler depth
disagrees between fields), instead of as model features — the brief's "filter, not feature" alternative.
Cost ~1 day if a gate pass is not needed first; bracket **0 to +0.008**, very low confidence; explicitly
dependent on H40's screen outcome (if dense persistence is inert, filtering on it inherits the verdict;
Euler depth (H39) remains independent). External data: none.
