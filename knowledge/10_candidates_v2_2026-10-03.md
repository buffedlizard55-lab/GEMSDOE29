# Candidate hypotheses v2 — five geological routes to faults the catalogue is missing

**Date:** 2026-10-03 (UTC). Ranked by expected DTI improvement per unit of implementation cost, after
deduplicating against every hypothesis previously implemented, registered or blocked in this repository
and in the reviewed sibling repositories (`GEMSDOE..GEMSDOE27`, `GEMSDOE24`, `GEMSDOE25`). “Not tried”
means the *specific construction* was not found in the reviewed repositories; it is not a claim about any
other competitor. Planning brackets are judgements, **not** predictions, and not observed results.

**Target definition used throughout** (see `knowledge/07_metric_emission_analysis_2026-10-03.md` §1):
the scored truth is *faults absent from the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue*, manually identified by experts, and
catalogue pixels are reported to be excluded from evaluation. A hypothesis therefore has to add faults the
catalogue lacks — a construction that mostly re-draws mapped traces is worthless by design.

## Rank 1 — H35: Structural-interaction-zone field (relay ramps, tips, junctions) with stress weighting

* **Layers.** Visible-catalogue geometry from the provided `labels.tif` / `existing_faults.tif` (masked
  pixels excluded from scoring but usable as input); LiDAR scarp descriptors `L_step_max`, `L_cross_max`,
  `L_coh100`, `L_strike_c2/s2`; potential-field ridges `A_mag_hg_ridge`, `A_grav_hg_ridge`; strain bands
  `geod_shearrate`, `geod_dilatationrate`, `geod_2ndinv`; radiometric ratios `D_ThK`, `D_UK`.
* **Signature.** Explicit *interaction-zone objects*: (i) overlapping sub-parallel trace tips within a
  relay-ramp window (step width ~0.5–3 km), (ii) trace terminations/horse-tails, (iii) intersections
  between normal faults and transverse oblique/strike-slip faults. Each object is scored by a
  **dilation-tendency** term (fault-plane strike/dip resolved against the regional stress field, using the
  standard normal/strike-slip tensors) and by independent-field corroboration (scarp step + coherence, a
  potential-field ridge, or a radiometric contrast). This is a *structural-geology* transform, not another
  edge filter.
* **Why it finds faults the catalogue lacks.** Great Basin inventories show geothermal systems concentrate
  where strain is distributed over multiple short, overlapping strands — step-overs/relay ramps ~32 % of
  catalogued systems (Faulds, Hinz & Kreemer, GDR 383) rising to ~47 % of Nevada producing systems in the
  2025 re-analysis (Giddens & Faulds, Stanford SGW 2025), terminations ~22–25 %, normal×transverse
  intersections ~22 %, while **major range-front faults host ~1 %**. Scarp-based catalogues trace the
  through-going strands and systematically miss exactly those short, overlapping, horsetailing strands —
  and newly mapped geometry of existing fault systems is what the organizer reports the hidden labels
  contain.
* **Difference from everything already implemented here.** Family E is distance/orientation/density around
  visible faults; `H27_tip` is a single-tip continuation; `H30-1` (paired-tip bridge × scarp) passed its
  screen but **failed fresh-draw confirmation**; the sibling registered a junction field (H30-2) but never
  fitted it. H35 differs in all three parts: relay-window *pairing* of tips, *dilation-tendency* weighting
  instead of distance-to-trace, and transverse-intersection objects.
* **Rank/cost/validation.** Rank 1: planning bracket **0 to +0.030 DTI**, low confidence, ~1–2 days.
  Validate on both proxies (`knowledge/09_h34_results_2026-10-03.md` §2): the catalogue-gap fold set *and*
  the state-geologic-map off-catalogue class, requiring ≥3/4 folds positive on the off-catalogue class and
  no loss > 0.005 on the catalogue class, with fresh draws.
* **External data (optional).** USGS per-segment slip/dilation tendency shapefile, Siler (2022),
  DOI [10.5066/P9YL58W6](https://doi.org/10.5066/P9YL58W6), ScienceBase item
  `6296974dd34ec53d276bb33d`. **Obtainability checked 2026-10-03:** the DOI/citation metadata resolve
  (verified through an independent index), but `sciencebase.gov` is unreachable from this sandbox
  (SSL_ERROR_SYSCALL) → the file must be fetched by the owner's unrestricted runner or a public mirror.
  The hypothesis is implementable **without** it (dilation tendency computed from geometry + regional
  stress); the USGS file is a cross-check, not a prerequisite.

## Rank 2 — H36: Magnetotelluric conductance *structural* edges and cross-fault asymmetry

* **Layers.** `cond_surf` (integrated conductance), `depth_to_base_surf` (depth to the conductive base),
  `tc`; corroborating ridges from family A.
* **Signature.** Edge transform on the conductance surfaces (ridge of |∇cond| with across-edge
  non-maximum suppression) plus a **step-asymmetry test** — a discrete conductance step across the edge,
  not a smooth gradient — and coherence of that edge with a potential-field lineament. In this USGS
  MT-derived model, conductance contrasts trace clay alteration and fluid pathways.
* **Why it finds faults the catalogue lacks.** MT sees through cover: a fault that is blind at the surface
  but still controls fluid flow generates a subsurface conductivity boundary with no scarp, so a
  scarp-based catalogue cannot contain it. It is also directly geothermal-relevant: alteration clay needs
  fluid, and fluid needs permeability.
* **Difference.** The repository carries raw gradients of these bands (`D_cond_grad`,
  `D_depth_base_grad`) as features and the sibling's factorial found the thermal/geochemical family
  “inert” **as tested** — raw bands and ratios. No reviewed repository applies a structural-edge plus
  asymmetry transform to the MT surfaces.
* **Rank/cost/validation.** Rank 2: planning bracket **0 to +0.010 DTI**, low confidence, ~0.5–1 day.
  Same dual-proxy validation. Cross-check layer (optional): Peacock & Bedrosian (2022) USGS
  electrical-conductance maps, DOI [10.5066/P9TWT2LU](https://doi.org/10.5066/P9TWT2LU) — again not
  reachable from this sandbox.

## Rank 3 — H37: Geothermometry residual (silica / chalcedony / Na-K) and spring-cluster geometry

* **Layers.** The hash-pinned GDR 1391 “Well and Spring Temperature and Chemistry” CSV already restored
  here (`temp_c`, `geothermquartz_c`, `geothermchalc_c`, `geothermcat_c`; SHA-256 `122718e65bdf…`,
  2,146,771 B, verified this session), radiometric heat-production ratios (K, Th, U), MT depth-to-base.
* **Signature.** Kernel-smoothed residual of *chemistry-derived reservoir temperature* (the silica and
  chalcedony geothermometers already tabulated in the file) against the regional heat-flow trend, weighted
  by spring/well clustering and restricted to off-catalogue lineament habitat. Chemistry-derived
  temperatures are far more informative than 2 m air-affected probe temperatures.
* **Why it finds faults the catalogue lacks.** A heat-flow anomaly with **no** mapped fault is the classic
  signature of a blind system: heat requires a permeable pathway, and the pathway is a fault that mapping
  missed. This is the mechanism INGENIOUS was funded to test.
* **Difference.** H29-2 (this repo) used residualized *2 m probe temperature* and failed its registered
  catalogue-proxy gate; the sibling's H27-2 was blocked on the probe binary. No reviewed repository uses
  the **chemistry-derived geothermometer columns** as a spatial field, and none intersects them with
  off-catalogue lineament habitat.
* **Rank/cost/validation.** Rank 3: planning bracket **0 to +0.015 DTI**, very low confidence (prior
  negative on the naive temperature version), ~1 day. Dual-proxy validation; the off-catalogue proxy is
  the one that matters for this hypothesis.

## Rank 4 — H38: Worm convergence and cross-field azimuth consistency

* **Layers.** `rtp` (magnetic) and `iso_grav_anom` (isostatic gravity), via the continuation ladder in
  `src/gemsdoe/worms.py` (heights 0–1200 m already registered for H31).
* **Signature.** Two new derivations of the existing ladder: (a) **convergence size** — how many 0-m edge
  seeds merge into one worm at the top of the ladder (a large basin of attraction is evidence of a single
  deep, laterally extensive source rather than a shallow local anomaly); (b) **azimuth agreement** between
  the magnetic and gravity gradient directions within 300 m (a real buried structure produces coherent
  gradient azimuths in both fields; acquisition lineation and cultural noise do not).
* **Why it finds faults the catalogue lacks.** Blind faults keep a coherent deep potential-field edge with
  no surface expression; convergence plus cross-field azimuth coherence is a stronger discriminator
  against noise than edge amplitude or persistence alone.
* **Difference.** H29 tested persistence as gate/rank/feature and a line-orientation audit; H31 tests
  pseudogravity persistence and lateral drift with a symmetric co-location term. Neither tests convergence
  statistics nor *directional* agreement. Flagged: a repository-wide grep for “azimuth/convergence” must
  be repeated before this is described as novel to the family.
* **Rank/cost/validation.** Rank 4: planning bracket **0 to +0.008 DTI**, very low confidence (prior
  negatives on related persistence variants), ~1 day. Note the H29 finding that E–W (survey-line-parallel)
  edges were the *most* persistent on this grid, which weakens the naive “aliasing dies under
  continuation” expectation but not the azimuth-agreement test.

## Rank 5 — H39: Euler-deconvolution depth selection with two-field depth consistency

* **Layers.** `rtp`, `iso_grav_anom` (and the H31 pseudogravity proxy as a cross-check); output is a
  per-edge depth estimate field.
* **Signature.** Window-based Euler deconvolution of the potential fields (structural index 0–1 for
  contacts/thin sheets after Reid et al., 1990, *Geophysics* 55, 80–91,
  [doi:10.1190/1.1442774](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.1442774)) producing an explicit depth per solution
  cluster; retain edges only where (i) the estimated depth is resolved and deep, (ii) magnetic and gravity
  depth estimates agree within tolerance, (iii) the edge lies off-catalogue.
* **Why it finds faults the catalogue lacks.** Explicit depth filtering removes shallow cultural and
  near-surface-conductor edges that a persistence proxy only weakly separates; a deep, two-field-consistent
  edge with no surface trace is a blind-fault candidate.
* **Difference.** H29/H31 use upward-continuation *persistence* as an implicit depth proxy; no reviewed
  repository computes Euler depths or a cross-field depth-agreement test.
* **Rank/cost/validation.** Rank 5: planning bracket **0 to +0.008 DTI**, very low confidence (Euler is
  sensitive to the structural index and window size; a mis-set SI produces confidently wrong depths),
  ~1–2 days. Dual-proxy validation; results must be reported with the SI/window sensitivity table.
