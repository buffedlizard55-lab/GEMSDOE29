## Evidence base and source audit

- The competition target is the presence of geological faults indicative of geothermal resources. The organizers state that the public fault catalogue is incomplete and may contain inaccurate data; the initial test contains expert-identified faults not in the public USGS database, and the same submissions are later re-scored against an expert-expanded set. Official source: [DrivenData problem description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/).
- The provided stack includes geodetic strain-rate fields, earthquake density, gravity, magnetics and terrain-related data. Official source: the same [problem description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/).
- GeoDAWN's official USGS ScienceBase release documents two survey areas, four acquisition blocks, E–W flight lines at 200 m (Area 1) or 400 m (Area 2) spacing, and variable terrain clearance. Source: [USGS ScienceBase, DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ](https://www.sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/657e1d85d34e23d3533209f7). This motivates an explicit spectral-artifact test; it does **not** imply that every edge parallel to a flight line is noise.
- INGENIOUS / GDR 1391 is publicly listed under CC BY 4.0 and exposes geothermal, structural, seismic, thermal and paleothermal resources. Source: [Geothermal Data Repository submission 1391](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391). Its landing page is reachable; direct shell retrieval of the paleothermal archive failed in this sandbox with `curl` SSL_ERROR_SYSCALL, so its bytes are *not* presently available for a reproducible local test.
- The method source is Hornby, Boschetti & Horowitz (1999), *Analysis of potential field data in the wavelet domain*, *Geophysical Journal International* 137(1), 175–196, DOI [10.1046/j.1365-246X.1999.00788.x](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-246X.1999.00788.x), verified against the publisher's [Oxford Academic record](https://academic.oup.com/gji/article/137/1/175/700677) and the [Stanford-hosted 2018 review](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/2018/Horowitz.pdf). Important qualification: upward-continuation persistence is a multiscale structural cue, **not** a unique depth estimate or a proof that an edge is a fault. Potential-field inversion is non-unique; signal amplitude, source geometry, interference and survey processing also affect persistence.

## Frozen first-test design: H29-5

1. **Candidate under test:** one explicit persistence × deformation × seismicity interaction added to a dense head; train only outside each held-out spatial quadrant plus the existing 15-pixel guard. No hidden labels may enter feature fitting or training.
2. **Spatial holdout:** the repo's four quadrant folds and draws 0–3, using the same catalogue-component hide-and-recover construction as the H29 screen. Report the catalogue-internal limitation next to every result.
3. **Budget and controls:** emit the same per-quadrant pixel budget as plain `dot_thin(H19-5, 2.8)` (A0). Compare paired candidate ΔDTI to A0 and, conservatively, to the best *same-fold / same-draw* pre-existing control among A0/A1/A2/B0/B1/B2. The best-control comparison is an eligibility check, not a public-score estimate.
4. **Gate:** mean paired ΔDTI ≥ +0.005 and at least 3/4 folds positive on **both** screen draws (0,1), and the same thresholds on at least one confirmation draw (2 or 3). Otherwise **FAIL**; no full-map slot candidate and no weekly submission slot.
5. **No live-slot action:** a local proxy PASS would only qualify the idea for a later owner-reviewed decision. Only the owner may upload, under the competition rules; no portal action is automated here.
6. **Known proxy limitation:** all hidden truth in this design comes from the public catalogue. It does not validate performance on faults absent from the catalogue; a negative result is “not eligible on this proxy,” not proof that the geologic mechanism is false.
